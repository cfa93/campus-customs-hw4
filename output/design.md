# Campus Customs — Design Notes

A navy-and-cream campus merch look, with bold Fraunces headings and a clean Inter
body. Each change below is meant to keep shoppers browsing longer and nudge them
toward buying. Examples are from the live site.

## Palette: navy + cream + gold
Cream page background, navy for the navbar, hero, footer and headings, and a warm
gold accent for prices, links and calls to action. **Why it helps:** it reads as
official Yale campus merch at a glance, which builds trust, and the calm cream
background keeps attention on the products instead of fighting them.

## Type: bold headings, clean body
Headings use Fraunces (e.g. the About page's "Made for Bulldogs by their
neighbors."); body copy uses Inter. **Why it helps:** big confident headings give
each page a clear focal point and a premium feel, while the clean body font keeps
descriptions easy to skim — shoppers get the gist fast and stay.

## Standout homepage hero
"Wear your Yale every day." now sits on a deep navy panel with a soft gold glow, a
gold eyebrow, and two clear buttons (gold "Shop the collection", outlined "Our
story"). **Why it helps:** a strong hero sets the tone and points straight to the
catalogue, so first-time visitors have an obvious next step instead of bouncing.

## Roomier product photos
Product cards now use a taller 4:5 white image area with padding, on a larger grid.
**Why it helps:** the garment is the thing people buy, so giving the photo more room
makes items look better and easier to judge, which lifts clicks into product pages.

## Subtle hover animations
Cards lift with a shadow and gold border and the photo zooms slightly; buttons lift
and turn gold on hover. **Why it helps:** gentle motion signals "this is clickable"
and makes browsing feel responsive and polished, encouraging more clicks without
being distracting.

## Chat styled as part of the store
The assistant uses rounded bubbles — navy for the shopper, cream for the bot — a navy
header, and product mini-cards that match the storefront. **Why it helps:** when the
chat looks built-in rather than bolted-on, shoppers trust it and actually use it for
help, and its product cards lead straight back to buying.

## Mobile layout
On small screens the navbar wraps with the links on their own row, the hero padding
shrinks, product detail stacks to one column, and the size grid drops to two columns.
**Why it helps:** most shopping traffic is on phones; a layout that stays clean and
tappable means those visitors keep browsing instead of leaving.
